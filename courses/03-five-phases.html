Five phases 五行: the correspondence engine

Wood, fire, earth, metal, water - and with them an organ, a season, an emotion, a taste, a colour and a direction each. This is the classification system that organizes the whole tradition, plus the two cycles that make it move. You will spend half this session on the live wheel, and watch the system patch its own arithmetic in real time.

Part 0

Why this session exists

Yin-yang gave you a two-way sort. Five phases gives you a five-way one, and that is where Chinese medicine gets its distinctive texture: the reason a practitioner might ask about your temper, your taste preferences and the season your symptoms worsen, all in one breath. Understand the correspondence engine and huge amounts of Chinese medicine, cooking, martial arts and fortune-telling suddenly share one grammar. Today you learn the grid, the two cycles, and the honest limits of both.

Part 1 · covers the concept and its translation

Not elements. Phases. 5 min live

五行 wǔxíng is usually rendered "five elements," and that translation quietly imports the wrong idea. The Greek stoicheia were substances - unchanging stuff the world is built from. 行 xíng means to walk, to move, to go. The Chinese terms are about process and change, not building material.

LiveThe translation fight (and why it is worth two minutes)2.5 min▶

There is no settled English word, and the disagreement is informative:

  • Manfred Porkert proposed "evolutive phase" - awkward, but it foregrounds change.
  • Marc Kalinowski's "five agents" is arguably the most accepted among working sinologists, and the Stanford Encyclopedia uses "Five Agents" and "Five Powers."
  • Joseph Needham is the standard reference point for the whole debate.
  • One wrinkle: some argue "phase" actually fits 五运 wǔyùn better than 五行, so "agents" may be the cleaner choice.

This course says "phases" because it is the most common serious rendering and it carries the process idea. But do not claim scholars are unanimous - they agree only that "elements" is misleading.

Real world

Why the word choice changes what you expect. If you hear "elements," you look for five substances in the body and find none, and the system looks like bad chemistry. If you hear "phases," you look for five modes of behaviour - expanding, peaking, stabilizing, contracting, storing - and the system looks like what it is: a way of sorting how things change over a cycle. Same doctrine, completely different first impression.

Self-studyWhere the doctrine sits in the text1 min read▶

The great correspondence chapter is 素问 ch.5 阴阳应象大论 - the same chapter that gave you the yin-yang thesis statement in Session 2. It lays out east, south, centre, west and north, each with its climatic qi, phase, zang organ, tissue, orifice, colour, taste and emotion, in a long recurring pattern. Read three paragraphs of it and the structure becomes obvious even without Chinese: the sentences rhyme structurally.

Note what that means: yin-yang and five phases arrive in the same chapter, welded together. They were originally separate Warring States schemes, fused into one medical cosmology by the Han.

Part 2 · the build-along · everyone clicks

The five-phases wheel ✦ 20 min hands-on

Here is the system as a live object. Click any phase to see everything the tradition files under it; toggle the two cycles to see how the phases act on each other. One honest note, stated in the tool itself: this visualizes the model. It asserts no physiology. The wheel is an accurate map of what the text and tradition claim, which is a different thing from a claim being true.

Tour the five. Click each phase in turn and read its row set. Notice how tightly the associations hang together: wood is spring, green, sour, the liver, and anger; water is winter, black, salty, the kidney, and fear. Whether or not it is true, it is memorable - which is exactly what a pre-print classification system needs to be.

Turn on 相生 shēng, the generating cycle. The green arrows run around the rim: wood feeds fire, fire makes earth (ash), earth bears metal (ore), metal enriches water (condensation on a blade, in the classical image), water feeds wood. Each phase produces the next. Say the loop out loud twice - it is easier as a story than as a list.

Now turn on 相克 kè, the controlling cycle. The red arrows cut across as a five-pointed star: wood breaks earth (roots), earth dams water, water quenches fire, fire melts metal, metal cuts wood. Each phase restrains the one two steps ahead.

Turn both on at once. This is the picture worth keeping: every phase feeds one neighbour and checks another, and is itself fed and checked. That mutual restraint is the model's whole idea of balance - and it is why a practitioner reasoning inside this system might treat one organ to affect another.

Copy the grid into your map. Five columns, and for now just three rows: zang organ, season, emotion. You will add the rest in a moment.

Memorize the shapes, not the lists Generating goes around the circle; controlling goes across the star. Two shapes, ten relationships. Every classical formulation you will ever meet is one of these arrows with a name attached.
Part 3 · covers the full grid and its seams

The full table, and where it strains 7 min live

The wheel showed you seven rows. The classical grid runs deeper, and two entries in it are worth flagging honestly - one because textbooks quietly disagree with the Neijing, and one because the system needed a fifth season it did not have.

青 qīng in the colour row is worth a note: it covers a range English splits into green and blue, which is why you will see both translations. It is the colour of spring growth and of distant mountains.

LiveSeam 1 · the lung's emotion, where textbooks drift2 min▶

The text says 在志为忧 - the emotion filed under metal and the lung is 忧 yōu, worry or melancholy (Suwen ch.5).

Tradition holds, in most modern textbooks, that it is 悲 bēi, grief.

Both circulate widely and the difference is small, but the habit matters: when a source and a textbook disagree, say so rather than picking silently. This course names both every time it comes up, and so should your map.

LiveSeam 2 · the fifth season that had to be invented3 min▶

Here is the best teaching moment in the whole correspondence system. The scheme needs five of everything. The year has four seasons. That is an arithmetic problem, and you can watch the tradition solve it.

  • In Suwen ch.5, the great correspondence chapter, earth is not given a season at all - it is given 中央 the centre, alongside the four directions. Four seasons, four directions, plus a middle. Clean.
  • In Suwen ch.22 藏气法时论, a different chapter, we get 脾主长夏 - "the spleen governs 长夏 chángxià, late summer." A fifth season appears, wedged between summer and autumn, to give earth its slot.

So the familiar five-season table is a patch, assembled from two different chapters that solve the problem two different ways. Nothing here is hidden or embarrassing - it is visible in the primary text to anyone who reads both chapters.

Real world

Why this is the honest reader's favourite moment. A system that patches itself in public is telling you what kind of system it is: one built by fitting phenomena into a scheme, rather than one built by deriving a scheme from phenomena. You will meet the same move in Session 4, when the body turns out to have five zang but needs six fu - and a sixth organ appears that the tradition itself says has no form.

Self-studyThe cycles applied to feelings1.5 min read▶

Suwen ch.5 runs the controlling cycle straight through the emotions, in a series of compact lines: 怒伤肝，悲胜怒 ("anger injures the liver; grief overcomes anger"), 思伤脾，怒胜思 ("pensiveness injures the spleen; anger overcomes pensiveness"), 喜伤心，恐胜喜 ("joy injures the heart; fear overcomes joy").

Read as psychology this is strange. Read as the grid being applied consistently, it is exactly what the system predicts: whatever controls wood controls anger, because anger is filed under wood. The doctrine is being generated by the table, not observed in patients. That is worth noticing every time you meet a correspondence claim.

Read & apply · 🧩 map step 3

Fill your grid ✦ 8 min · builds your map

Your Neijing map has five phase columns waiting. Now they get filled - by hand, because copying a table by hand is how a table becomes memory.

Write the ten rows from the table above into your five columns. Chinese character plus your own English gloss. Do not photograph it - write it.

Draw both cycles in the margin as a single pentagon: rim arrows for 相生, star arrows for 相克. Two shapes, no lists.

Mark the two seams in a different colour: "忧 in Suwen 5, 悲 in textbooks" next to the metal emotion, and "长夏 is from ch.22 - ch.5 says 中央" next to the earth season. Those two annotations are what separate your map from a wellness infographic.

Add your journal line. Then, for interest only, look at today's entry and note which phase's season you are actually in. Session 5 will ask what the text prescribes for it.

Homework

Before Session 4 ◐ 20-25 min total

  • Recite both cycles from the shapes - rim, then star - without looking. Two minutes a day for three days and they are yours permanently.
  • Spot the grid in the wild. Find one claim online of the form "eat [colour/taste] food for your [organ]." Trace it back through the table and identify which correspondence row generated it. Label it: tradition holds - a table inference, not a Neijing sentence and not a trial.
  • Journal daily - one line, three words.
  • Optional: skim Suwen ch.5 on ctext.org and find the repeating east/south/centre/west/north paragraphs. You will recognize the structure now even without reading Chinese.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The 相生 generating cycle runs...

Generating goes around the rim: wood feeds fire, fire makes ash (earth), earth bears metal, metal enriches water, water feeds wood. Option A is the controlling star.

2 · 长夏 "late summer" as the earth season comes from...

Five phases, four seasons. Ch.5 solves it with 中央 the centre; ch.22 solves it by inventing a fifth season. The familiar table is a patch stitched from both.

3 · "Five elements" is a poor translation of 五行 because...

Greek elements were building material; 五行 sorts modes of behaviour and change. Scholars agree "elements" misleads - they disagree between phases, agents and powers.
