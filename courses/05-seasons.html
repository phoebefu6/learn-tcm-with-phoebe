Living with the seasons: 四气调神

Suwen chapter 2 tells you when to sleep, how to walk, what to do with your will and your temper, four times over - once per season. It is the most immediately usable chapter in the book and the most plundered by wellness marketing. Today: what it actually prescribes, in its own words, plus a prevention argument that reads startlingly modern and a famous quotation that is not from this book at all.

Part 0

Why this session exists

Everything so far has been structure: the book, the two-way sort, the five-way grid, the twelve offices. Chapter 2 of the Suwen is where the system turns into instructions. 四气调神大论 - "the great treatise on attuning the spirit to the four qi" - gives four parallel paragraphs, one per season, each telling you when to sleep, how to move, and what emotional posture to hold. It is short, it is concrete, and it is quoted more often and more loosely than any other chapter in the canon. So we will read it properly, then read what people claim it says.

Part 1 · covers Suwen ch.2, the heart of the session

The four regimens 14 min live

Four paragraphs, structurally identical. Each opens by naming the season's three months and giving it a two-character motto, then prescribes sleep, then bodily activity, then what to do with 志 zhì, the will - and closes by naming the "way" that season is nurturing. Read the four in sequence and the argument arrives before the explanation does.

LiveSpring and summer, in the text's own words3 min▶
✦ 春三月 · Suwen ch.2春三月，此謂發陳。夜臥早起，廣步於庭，被髮緩形，以使志生，生而勿殺，予而勿奪，賞而勿罰。 "The three months of spring: this is called bringing forth the old. Go to bed late and rise early; walk broadly in the courtyard, loosen the hair and let the body relax, so as to let the will generate. Generate and do not kill, give and do not take away, reward and do not punish." This, the chapter says, is 養生之道 - the way of nurturing birth.
✦ 夏三月 · Suwen ch.2夏三月，此謂蕃秀。夜臥早起，無厭於日，使志無怒，使華英成秀，使氣得泄，若所愛在外。 "The three months of summer: this is called luxuriant flowering. Go to bed late and rise early; do not resent the long day. Keep the will free of anger, let the blossoms come to splendour, let the qi get out - as if what you love were outside." This is 養長 - nurturing growth.

Two things to notice. First, the sleep instruction is identical across both: 夜臥早起. Spring and summer are the short-night half of the year, and the chapter simply says so. Second, the emotional instructions are not vague uplift - they are specific and social. Spring says give, reward, do not punish. Summer says do not be angry, and let what you love pull you outward. A Han reader would have heard a governance metaphor as much as a health one.

LiveAutumn and winter, in the text's own words3 min▶
✦ 秋三月 · Suwen ch.2秋三月，此謂容平。早臥早起，與雞俱興，使志安寧，以緩秋刑，收斂神氣。 "The three months of autumn: this is called containment and levelling. Go to bed early and rise early, up together with the chickens. Keep the will peaceful and calm, so as to soften autumn's executions; draw the spirit-qi inward and gather it." This is 養收 - nurturing gathering.
✦ 冬三月 · Suwen ch.2冬三月，此謂閉藏。早臥晚起，必待日光，使志若伏若匿，若有私意，若已有得，去寒就溫，無泄皮膚。 "The three months of winter: this is called closing and storing. Go to bed early and rise late; you must wait for the daylight. Let the will lie low and stay hidden, as if you had a private thought, as if you had already got what you wanted. Leave the cold and go toward warmth; do not let the skin leak." This is 養藏 - nurturing storage.

秋刑 "autumn's executions" is a real Han image, not a translator's flourish: autumn was the season for judicial killing, the sky's own severity made law. The chapter asks you to soften it in yourself. And winter's simile is the strangest and best line in the chapter - hold your will the way you would hold a secret, or a thing you have already been given and no longer need to chase.

LiveThe sleep gradient is the chapter in miniature3 min▶

If you remember one thing from Suwen ch.2, make it this. Line the four sleep instructions up:

  • 春 夜臥早起 - late to bed, rise early
  • 夏 夜臥早起 - late to bed, rise early (same as spring)
  • 秋 早臥早起 - early to bed, early up
  • 冬 早臥晚起 - early to bed, rise late

The night gets longer, one step at a time, and only winter says rise late. Three of the four seasons say rise early; the one exception is the one where the chapter also says 必待日光, wait for the daylight. The gradient tracks the daylight, and the whole chapter is that idea worked out across sleep, activity, and mood.

Real world

This is the line wellness copy gets wrong most often. "The Neijing says go to bed early and rise early" is a sentence you will meet constantly. The text says that for autumn only. For spring and summer it says the opposite about bedtime, and for winter it says the opposite about waking. Four paragraphs, four different answers - and a quotation that flattens them into one is not quoting the chapter, it is quoting a slogan.

Self-studyWhy the four verbs matter more than the four to-do lists1.5 min read▶

養生 · 養長 · 養收 · 養藏. Birth, growth, gathering, storage. Strip out every specific instruction and this is what remains: a single arc, four positions on it, and the claim that a person should sit at the same position the year is sitting at.

That is the yin-yang argument from Session 2 in practical dress - the expanding half of the year and the contracting half, each with an early and a late phase. It is also why 養生 yǎngshēng, "nurturing life," became the name of an entire genre of Chinese self-cultivation literature stretching well beyond medicine. The word you will see on the cover of a modern Chinese health magazine comes straight out of this paragraph.

Part 2 · covers the prevention passage and a famous misattribution

治未病 - treating the not-yet-ill 10 min live

The same chapter ends with the single most quoted idea in Chinese medicine, and it is a genuinely good one. It is also the idea most often quoted with the wrong source attached - by wellness sites, by textbooks, and, as you are about to see, by the theoretical journal of the Chinese Communist Party. This is the best source-checking lesson in the course.

✦ 素问 · 四气调神大论 (Suwen ch.2)是故聖人不治已病治未病，不治已亂治未亂，此之謂也。夫病已成而後藥之，亂已成而後治之，譬猶渴而穿井，鬥而鑄錐，不亦晚乎！ Shì gù shèngrén bù zhì yǐ bìng zhì wèi bìng, bù zhì yǐ luàn zhì wèi luàn, cǐ zhī wèi yě. Fú bìng yǐ chéng ér hòu yào zhī, luàn yǐ chéng ér hòu zhì zhī, pì yóu kě ér chuān jǐng, dòu ér zhù zhuī, bù yì wǎn hū! "Thus the sage does not treat what is already ill but treats what is not yet ill... To medicate a disease after it has formed is like digging a well when you are already thirsty, or casting a weapon when the fight has already started. Is that not too late?"
LiveThe well and the thirst3 min▶

The text says it, and it says it with two images rather than an argument: digging the well once you are thirsty, forging the weapon once the fight has started. Two thousand years later that is still the cleanest one-line case for preventive medicine anyone has made. It needs no cosmology to work - the simile carries it.

Note also the parallel structure the chapter is built on: 不治已病治未病 sits next to 不治已亂治未亂. Illness and disorder, the body and the state, the same sentence shape. The Neijing keeps analogizing the body to a polity, exactly as Session 4's twelve offices did.

A second version exists in the other half of the canon. 灵枢·逆顺 (Lingshu, "Contrary and Compliant") gives: 上工治未病，不治已病 - "the superior workman treats what is not yet ill; he does not treat what is already ill." 上工 is a craftsman's word, not a scholar's. Both places are real and both are worth citing; what you must not do is cite a third place that does not exist.

LiveThe famous three-tier saying is not from this book4 min▶

You have almost certainly met this line, usually captioned "as the Huangdi Neijing says":

✦ The saying, and its actual source上医治未病之病，中医治欲病之病，下医治已病之病。 "The superior physician treats the illness that is not yet ill; the middling physician treats the illness that is about to be ill; the inferior physician treats the illness that is already ill." SOURCE: 孙思邈 Sun Simiao, 《备急千金要方·卷第一·论诊候第四》 - Tang dynasty. NOT the Neijing. ORIGINAL VERB: 医 not 治. "上医医未病之病，中医医欲病之病，下医医已病之病." PRECEDED BY: 古之善为医者，上医医国，中医医人，下医医病。 "Of the ancients who were good at medicine: the superior treated the state, the middling treated the person, the inferior treated the disease."

So the three-tier version is Tang, roughly 700 years after the Neijing layers, and even the verb has drifted in transmission: Sun Simiao wrote 医 (to doctor), and the version in circulation says 治 (to treat). Note also what the preceding sentence does - it puts the physician of the state above the physician of the person. The passage is as much political as clinical.

There is an intermediate step, and it is narrower than either. 《难经·七十七难》 - the Nanjing, the same text that gave Session 4 its 有名而无形 line about the sanjiao - has a two-tier 上工/中工 version. But read what it actually means:

  • 见肝之病，则知肝当传之与脾，故先实其脾气 - "seeing an illness of the liver, one knows the liver will transmit it to the spleen, and so first replenishes the spleen's qi."
  • That is pre-empting transmission between organs in an already-sick patient, using the controlling cycle from Session 3. It is not "prevent illness in the healthy."

Three texts, three different ideas, routinely collapsed into one quotation with one wrong attribution.

LiveThe kicker: the misattribution runs all the way to the top3 min▶

This is not a wellness-blog problem. In 2020, an article on 求是网 - the website of 《求是》, the theoretical journal of the Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party - stated: 《黄帝内经》有载，"上医治未病，中医治欲病，下医治已病" - "the Huangdi Neijing records: the superior physician treats the not-yet-ill..."

That sentence does not appear in the Neijing. It appears, in a slightly different form and with a different verb, in Sun Simiao's Tang-dynasty formulary.

Real world

Why this is the best exercise in the course. The claim is not fringe, not malicious, and not even wrong about the idea - the Neijing genuinely does teach 治未病, in the passage you read above. It is simply the wrong sentence with the wrong source. And it survives at that altitude because nobody checks: the quotation sounds classical, the source sounds obvious, and the primary text is free online in thirty seconds. Show the claim, then show the primary texts. That habit is worth more than any fact in this course.

The honest summary you can defend anywhere: the Neijing teaches 治未病 (Suwen ch.2, plus 灵枢·逆顺). The three-tier 上医/中医/下医 formulation is Sun Simiao. The organ-transmission version is the Nanjing. Three sources, kept separate.

Self-studyHow to check a classical quotation in ninety seconds1 min read▶
  • Take the most distinctive four to six characters of the claim - not the whole sentence, since transmitted texts vary.
  • Search them on ctext.org, which carries the Neijing, the Nanjing and a great deal more with full-text search.
  • If the phrase appears, note the chapter and quote it with the chapter. If it does not, search the phrase alone and see which text it actually belongs to.
  • Then ask the second question, the one people skip: does the source it belongs to mean what the quoter says it means? The Nanjing 77 case above passes the first test and fails the second.
Part 3 · covers what to make of the regimens

Reading the regimens honestly 6 min live

Chapter 2 is where a reader most wants to say "see, they were right." Resist it for one more session. There is a real and interesting question about seasonal light and sleep timing, and there is a separate question about whether a Han cosmology anticipated it. Keeping them apart is the whole discipline of this course.

LiveThe convergence, stated without the overclaim3 min▶

Here is the neutral version. Seasonal variation in light exposure and in sleep timing are real phenomena, and modern chronobiology studies them. Suwen ch.2 prescribes a sleep schedule that varies by season and explicitly ties winter waking to daylight (必待日光). Those two sentences are both true, and putting them next to each other is fair.

What is not fair is the third sentence people add: "so the Neijing anticipated chronobiology." It did not, and here is the precise reason - the chapter's reason for the schedule is the seasonal movement of qi within a correlative cosmology, not an observation about light and physiology. A system can produce an instruction that overlaps with a later finding without containing the finding. Correct output, unrelated derivation.

The label to use, every time The text says X - sleep this way in this season. Whether X is beneficial to health is a separate empirical question, answered by studies, not by chapters, and this course does not settle it. Session 6 shows you what that kind of answer looks like when it exists: named studies, exact effect sizes, stated confidence intervals.
LiveThe chapter 1 formula, revisited2 min▶

You met this in Session 1, and it belongs here too. Suwen ch.1 上古天真论 gives the general version of the same argument: 食飲有節，起居有常，不妄作勞 - moderate in food and drink, regular in rising and resting, not exhausting oneself with reckless labour.

Strip the cosmology and a modern doctor would sign it. That is genuinely interesting - and it is not evidence that the cosmology is right. A tradition can arrive at sensible general advice by many routes, including simple long observation of what wrecks people, and then explain it with whatever theory it has to hand. The advice and the explanation are separable, and in this text they came bundled.

Notice too what ch.1 pairs the sensible items with: 法於陰陽 "pattern yourself on yin and yang" and 和於術數 "accord with the arts of calculation." Those are in the same sentence, doing cosmological work. The text does not offer you the modern-sounding half on its own.

LiveWhat the wellness industry does with this chapter1.5 min▶

Suwen ch.2 is the single most commercially useful chapter in the canon, because it is short, concrete, and organized by a calendar that repeats forever. So you will see it quoted to sell:

  • seasonal detox programmes and "spring liver cleanses" - the liver-spring pairing is the Session 3 correspondence grid, not a chapter 2 instruction, and "cleanse" is not a category the text has;
  • seasonal supplement bundles, teas and 膏方-style tonics timed to the four seasons;
  • "sleep by the Neijing" schedules that quote one sleep rule as if it were four.

Label those honestly: marketing. Not "the text says," not "tradition holds" - a product pitch that has borrowed a classical sentence for authority. And note the structural point that makes the tell easy: chapter 2 prescribes behaviour, not products. Sleep, walking, hair, warmth, temper. There is nothing in it to buy.

The standing rail, again, because this is where it matters most Nothing in this session is a health recommendation. If you have a live health question, the NCCIH line applies: "Don't use TCM to replace or delay seeking conventional care."
Read & apply 1 of 2

Your journal against the chapter ✦ 10 min · everyone does this

This is the moment the one-line journal you started in Session 1 pays for itself. You are going to put a Han-dynasty prescription next to your own recorded behaviour and look at the gap. Framed exactly: this is a reading exercise, not a treatment plan. You are not being asked to comply with anything. You are being asked to observe.

Open the journal and find the current season. Read your last two or three weeks of entries - date, season, three words about sleep, energy and mood. Do not tidy them.

Read this season's paragraph above, slowly. Write down what it actually prescribes, in four items: sleep, activity, emotional posture, and the 養 verb. Use the text's wording, not a paraphrase from memory.

Put them side by side. What does the text prescribe? What did you actually do? Mark each of the four items agree, diverge, or not applicable. Be accurate rather than flattering - "the text says rise early and I have not seen a sunrise since March" is a better entry than a rounded-up one.

Then the interesting question. Where you diverge - does it bother you at all? Some divergences will feel like nothing (loosening your hair in the courtyard). Some may land (winter's "wait for the daylight"). Note which is which. That reaction is data about you, not evidence about the text.

Write one sentence on what you noticed, and stop there. No plan, no resolutions, no schedule change. The exercise is complete when you have looked.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which season does Suwen ch.2 tell you to rise late?

Spring and summer share 夜臥早起; autumn is 早臥早起; winter alone is 早臥晚起，必待日光. That one exception is the whole gradient, and it is the clause wellness copy flattens most often.

2 · 聖人不治已病治未病 - "the sage treats what is not yet ill" - comes from...

It closes the four-seasons chapter, with the well-and-thirst simile attached. The Lingshu 逆顺 version reads 上工治未病，不治已病. Both are genuine Neijing passages - cite either, with the chapter.

3 · "上医治未病之病，中医治欲病之病，下医治已病之病" was written by...

And his verb was 医, not 治. The misattribution is everywhere, including a 2020 求是网 article that credits it to the Neijing. Show the claim, then show the primary text.
