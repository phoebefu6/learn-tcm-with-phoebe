# learn-tcm-with-phoebe - source + coverage map

"Reading the Yellow Emperor" - a guided first read of 黄帝内经 for curious modern readers.
6 sessions, single track, running project = the learner's personal Neijing map + seasonal
journal. Signature interactive: tcm-live.js five-phases wheel. Research verified 2026-08-06.

## The honesty rail (strictest of any course in the shelf)

Three labels, used explicitly on every substantive claim:
- **"The text says"** - what the Neijing itself states, with chapter attribution. Primary source.
- **"Tradition holds"** - later systematization or received practice, NOT in the Neijing.
- **"Research says"** - modern evidence with exact numbers and study names. Used sparingly, never blurred with the above.

Plus a standing rail on every page: **this is a reading course about a foundational classic
and a living tradition. Not medical advice. NCCIH's line, quoted directly: "Don't use TCM to
replace or delay seeking conventional care."**

## Verified fact sheet (exact - use as written)

### Session 1: the book
- 素问 Suwen + 灵枢 Lingshu, **81 chapters each, 162 total** (ctext.org TOC).
- Q&A dialogue: 黄帝问曰 / 岐伯对曰. Other interlocutors: 雷公, 伯高, 少俞, 鬼臾区.
- Stratified compilation, NOT one author. SEP: "complex and multi-layered text," "probably compiled in the first century BCE," "a composite of several earlier texts."
- Unschuld's dating: language + ideas of the Suwen composed **c. 400 BCE - 260 CE**.
- Unschuld's sharper point: **no surviving version is identical to the Han-dynasty text of the same name**. Every extant text is the last of a long series of recompilations.
- 黄帝 = legendary culture hero; attribution to him was a standard Han authority convention (黄老 corpus works the same way).
- **王冰 Wang Bing, 762 CE**, after 12 years: gathered fragments, **reorganized into the present 81-chapter form**, wrote his own additions **in red ink** - later copyists absorbed some into the main text, so parts of the "ancient" Suwen are demonstrably Tang. Best honesty beat in the course.
- Received text = 《重广补注黄帝内经素问》, **Song 校正医书局** (Bureau for Correcting Medical Texts), established **1057** (嘉祐二年) on 韩琦's proposal; editors 林亿, 掌禹锡, 苏颂, 高保衡, 孙奇, 孙兆. In the Suwen alone: **6,000+ characters corrected, 2,000+ annotations added**.
- Free full Chinese text: **ctext.org/huangdi-neijing** (both halves). Caveat: rate-limits + CAPTCHAs on heavy access. Backups: 古诗文网, 中医世家 zysj.com.cn, neijing.5000yan.com.
- **UNESCO Memory of the World, 2011**: the copy inscribed is the **1339 Yuan-dynasty woodblock edition by 胡氏古林书堂** (a PRIVATE commercial printer), held at the **National Library of China**. NOT a Ming edition, NOT the Gu Congde edition. Inscribed alongside 《本草纲目》 (1593 Jinling first edition) at the Manchester IAC session, May 2011.
- HEDGE: "81+81 has always been the structure" - the present arrangement is Wang Bing's 762 CE work.

### Session 2: yin-yang
**Suwen ch.5 阴阳应象大论 thesis passage (原文):**
> 陰陽者，天地之道也，萬物之綱紀，變化之父母，生殺之本始，神明之府也。

Pinyin: Yīnyáng zhě, tiāndì zhī dào yě, wànwù zhī gāngjì, biànhuà zhī fùmǔ, shēngshā zhī běnshǐ, shénmíng zhī fǔ yě.
English: "Yin and yang are the Way of heaven and earth, the guiding net of the ten thousand things, the father and mother of change and transformation, the root and beginning of generating and killing, the storehouse of spirit brightness."

Also ch.5: 陰陽者，血氣之男女也；左右者，陰陽之道路也；水火者，陰陽之徵兆也。 ("Yin and yang are the male and female of blood and qi; left and right are the pathways of yin and yang; water and fire are the visible signs of yin and yang.")

- Body applications: back=yang/front=yin; upper=yang/lower=yin; 表 exterior=yang/里 interior=yin; six fu=yang/five zang=yin; qi=yang/blood=yin.
- Mutual transformation is IN the text (重阴必阳，重阳必阴 formulations in ch.5); the whole four-seasons chapter is a yin-yang argument in practical dress.
- What it IS (scholarly): **correlative cosmology**, a Han classification technology. SEP: the text "analogizes the body, the state, and the cosmos in systems of 'correlative cosmology'"; yin-yang and wuxing function as **classification systems**. SEP quotes the text's own reasoning: "Heaven is round, earth is square; people's heads are round, their feet are square and thereby correspond to them."
- Unschuld's term: **"medicine of systematic correspondence."** It succeeded because it "resonated with the life worlds of the elite in pre-Han and Han China" and could absorb demonological disease models into naturalism - swapping evil climatic qi for malevolent spirits while keeping the expel-the-bad-thing logic.
- What it is NOT: an empirical physiological finding. Not derived from dissection or measurement; a pre-existing cosmological grammar applied to the body.
- Good teaching frame: a *language for relationships and change* that a Han intellectual would find as natural as we find "systems" or "feedback loops."

### Session 3: five phases (the wheel session)
- **Translation:** "elements" is misleading - Greek stoicheia were substances; 行 xíng concerns **process and change**. Scholars AGREE "elements" is wrong but DISAGREE on the replacement: Porkert = "evolutive phase," Kalinowski = "five agents" (arguably most accepted among sinologists), Needham = the standard reference point; SEP uses "Five Agents"/"Five Powers"; some argue "phase" fits 五运 better than 五行. Never claim unanimity.
- **Correspondence table** (verified against Suwen ch.5's east/south/centre/west/north scheme):

| | 木 Wood | 火 Fire | 土 Earth | 金 Metal | 水 Water |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zang 脏 | 肝 liver | 心 heart | 脾 spleen | 肺 lung | 肾 kidney |
| Fu 腑 | 胆 gallbladder | 小肠 sm. intestine | 胃 stomach | 大肠 lg. intestine | 膀胱 bladder |
| Season | 春 spring | 夏 summer | 长夏 late summer | 秋 autumn | 冬 winter |
| Direction | 东 east | 南 south | 中 centre | 西 west | 北 north |
| Climatic qi | 风 wind | 热/暑 heat | 湿 damp | 燥 dryness | 寒 cold |
| Emotion 志 | 怒 anger | 喜 joy | 思 pensiveness | 忧 worry | 恐 fear |
| Taste 味 | 酸 sour | 苦 bitter | 甘 sweet | 辛 acrid | 咸 salty |
| Colour 色 | 青 green-blue | 赤 red | 黄 yellow | 白 white | 黑 black |
| Tissue | 筋 sinews | 血/脉 vessels | 肉 flesh | 皮毛 skin+hair | 骨 bone |
| Orifice 窍 | 目 eyes | 舌 tongue | 口 mouth | 鼻 nose | 耳 ears |

- **相生 generating:** 木生火，火生土，土生金，金生水，水生木. Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water→Wood. (pentagon rim)
- **相克 controlling:** 木克土，土克水，水克火，火克金，金克木. Wood→Earth→Water→Fire→Metal→Wood. (pentagram)
- 生 = engendering/promoting/fostering; 克 = restraining/checking/inhibiting.
- **PRECISION 1:** Suwen ch.5 says the metal/lung emotion is **忧 worry** ("在志为忧"); modern textbooks often substitute **悲 grief**. Name both, do not pick silently.
- **PRECISION 2:** **长夏 late summer is NOT in ch.5** - ch.5 uses 中央/centre for earth. The spleen-late-summer pairing is **Suwen ch.22 藏气法时论**: 脾主长夏... 脾苦湿，急食苦以燥之。 The fifth season is a **patch for the four-seasons/five-phases arithmetic problem** - great teaching moment about a system patching itself.
- Ch.5 also applies the controlling cycle to emotions: 怒伤肝，悲胜怒 · 思伤脾，怒胜思 · 喜伤心，恐胜喜.

### Session 4: zang-fu + substances
**Suwen ch.11 五藏别论 - the functional definition (原文):**
> 所謂五藏者，藏精氣而不瀉也，故滿而不能實。六府者，傳化物而不藏，故實而不能滿也。

English: "The five zang store essence-qi and do not discharge it; therefore they can be full but not stuffed. The six fu transmit and transform substances but do not store; therefore they can be stuffed but not full." A **storage vs throughput** contrast, not an anatomical one.

- Five zang: 肝 心 脾 肺 肾. Six fu: 胆 胃 大肠 小肠 **三焦** 膀胱.
- Bonus: **奇恒之腑 "extraordinary fu"** (same chapter) - 脑髓骨脉胆女子胞 (brain, marrow, bone, vessels, gallbladder, uterus): store like zang, shaped like fu. Gallbladder is double-listed and the tradition notices.
- **Suwen ch.8 灵兰秘典论 - twelve organs as court officials.** Clearest proof organs are OFFICES/FUNCTIONS: 心者，君主之官也，神明出焉。肺者，相傅之官，治節出焉。肝者，將軍之官，謀慮出焉。膽者，中正之官，決斷出焉。脾胃者，倉廩之官，五味出焉。三焦者，決瀆之官，水道出焉。膀胱者，州都之官，津液藏焉，氣化則能出矣。 (Variant: 食廪之官 for spleen-stomach; use 倉廩.)
- **Suwen ch.23 宣明五气:** 心藏神，肺藏魄，肝藏魂，脾藏意，肾藏志。
- **The spleen example (honest version):** TCM 脾 is a functionally defined system - digestion, absorption, distribution - overlapping essentially nothing with the anatomical spleen's immune/haematological role. DO NOT say "the ancients thought the spleen digested food and were wrong." DO say: **translating 脾 with the English organ name "spleen" is a translation artefact that creates a false comparison.** Neijing's own term = 倉廩之官 (granary office); 主运化 "transformation and transportation" is the LATER systematized textbook formulation.
- **三焦 sanjiao - the best honest example:** counted as a fu, has **no anatomical counterpart**. WHO's nomenclature committee kept the untranslated Chinese term for exactly this reason. Neijing's contribution = 决渎之官，水道出焉 (ch.8). **有名而无形 "has a name but no form" is from 《难经》 Nanjing (ch.25/38), NOT the Neijing** - attribute correctly. Nanjing ch.38 poses it as arithmetic: "how is it there are five zang but six fu?" The system needed a sixth fu for yin-yang symmetry, got one, and is candid it has no form.
- **Lingshu ch.30 决气 - the six substances, with Huangdi asking the definitional question himself:** 余聞人有精、氣、津、液、血、脈，余意以為一氣耳，今乃辨為六名，余不知其所以然。 Answers: 精 essence ("two spirits join to form a shape, born before the body itself"), 气 qi ("upper burner opens and diffuses, spreading the flavours of the five grains... like the soaking of mist and dew"), 津 jin (thin - sweat-like), 液 ye (thick - joints, brain, marrow), 血 blood ("middle burner takes the juice and transforms it to red"), 脉 vessels ("dams and channels the ying qi so it has nowhere to escape").
- 津液 is usually one compound category; this chapter shows it merges two distinguished terms.
- 神 shen sits apart from the six: 心藏神 (ch.23) + 神明出焉 (ch.8).

### Session 5: the seasons
**Suwen ch.2 四气调神大论 - four regimens, verbatim:**

| Season | Text | Sleep/wake | Activity | Emotional posture | The "way" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 春三月 "此謂發陳" | 夜臥早起，廣步於庭，被髮緩形，以使志生，生而勿殺，予而勿奪，賞而勿罰 | late to bed, **rise early** | walk broadly in the courtyard; loosen hair, relax the body | let the will generate; give rather than take, reward rather than punish | 養生 nurturing birth |
| 夏三月 "此謂蕃秀" | 夜臥早起，無厭於日，使志無怒，使華英成秀，使氣得泄，若所愛在外 | late to bed, **rise early** | do not resent the long day | will free of anger; let qi vent outward, "as if what you love is outside" | 養長 nurturing growth |
| 秋三月 "此謂容平" | 早臥早起，與雞俱興，使志安寧，以緩秋刑，收斂神氣 | **early to bed, early up - with the chickens** | gather in | will tranquil, softening "autumn's executions"; draw spirit-qi inward | 養收 nurturing gathering |
| 冬三月 "此謂閉藏" | 早臥晚起，必待日光，使志若伏若匿，若有私意，若已有得，去寒就溫，無泄皮膚 | **early to bed, rise LATE - wait for daylight** | avoid cold, seek warmth; do not open the pores | let the will lie low and hidden, "as if you had a private thought, as if you had already got what you wanted" | 養藏 nurturing storage |

**Only winter says rise late.** That single sleep gradient is the chapter in miniature.

**治未病 - Suwen ch.2 (原文):**
> 是故聖人不治已病治未病，不治已亂治未亂，此之謂也。夫病已成而後藥之，亂已成而後治之，譬猶渴而穿井，鬥而鑄錐，不亦晚乎！

English: "Thus the sage does not treat what is already ill but treats what is not yet ill... To medicate a disease after it has formed is like digging a well when you are already thirsty, or casting a weapon when the fight has already started. Is that not too late?"

- Second version, **灵枢·逆顺**: 上工治未病，不治已病。 (上工 = superior *workman*.)
- **MISATTRIBUTION TO TEACH:** 上医治未病之病，中医治欲病之病，下医治已病之病 is **NOT the Neijing** - it is **孙思邈 Sun Simiao, 《备急千金要方·卷第一·论诊候第四》** (Tang), and the original verb is **医 not 治**: "上医医未病之病，中医医欲病之病，下医医已病之病," preceded by "古之善为医者，上医医国，中医医人，下医医病." Intermediate step: 《难经·七十七难》 has a two-tier 上工/中工 version with a NARROWER meaning - pre-empting disease transmission BETWEEN ORGANS (见肝之病，则知肝当传之与脾，故先实其脾气), not preventing illness in the healthy. **The misattribution runs to the top:** a 2020 求是网 (CPC theoretical journal) article states "《黄帝内经》有载，'上医治未病...'" - a sentence that does not appear in the Neijing. Show the claim, then the primary texts. Teaches source-checking better than any lecture.

**Suwen ch.1 上古天真论 passages:**
> 上古之人，其知道者，法於陰陽，和於術數，食飲有節，起居有常，不妄作勞，故能形與神俱，而盡終其天年，度百歲乃去。

English: "The people of high antiquity who knew the Way patterned themselves on yin and yang... were moderate in food and drink, regular in rising and resting, and did not exhaust themselves with reckless labour. So body and spirit stayed together, and they lived out their allotted years, departing only past a hundred."

> 今時之人不然也，以酒為漿，以妄為常，醉以入房... 起居無節，故半百而衰也。
> 恬惔虛无，真氣從之，精神內守，病安從來。 ("In calm detachment and emptiness, the true qi follows along; with essence and spirit guarded within, from where would illness come?")

### Session 6: the honest reckoning
**Acupuncture:**
- **Vickers et al 2012** (Arch Intern Med 172(19):1444-53), IPD meta-analysis, 29 RCTs, 17,922 patients. **vs sham**: back/neck pain SMD 0.23 (CI 0.13-0.33), osteoarthritis 0.16 (0.07-0.25), chronic headache 0.15 (0.07-0.24). **vs no-acupuncture**: 0.55, 0.57, 0.42. Verbatim: "Significant differences between true and sham acupuncture indicate that acupuncture is more than a placebo. However, these differences are relatively modest, suggesting that factors in addition to the specific effects of needling are important contributors."
- **Vickers 2018 update** (J Pain 19(5):455-474), 39 trials, 20,827 patients: ~0.5 SD vs no acupuncture, ~0.2 SD vs sham; ~15% decay at 1 year. (Use 2012 for per-condition numbers - a 2018 table was internally inconsistent.)
- **Cochrane migraine prophylaxis** (Linde 2016, 22 trials, 4,985 participants): vs sham SMD **-0.18** (-0.28 to -0.08), moderate quality, responders 50% vs 41%, **NNTB 11**. Conclusion: an effect over sham exists, "but this effect is small."
- **Cochrane tension-type headache** (Linde 2016): 51 vs 40 responders per 100 vs sham; ~2 fewer headache days/month; "little or no difference in headache intensity"; GRADE moderate.
- **NCCIH (Oct 2022):** "How acupuncture works is not fully understood"; may help back/neck pain, knee OA, postoperative pain; "the difference between acupuncture and no treatment was greater than the difference between acupuncture and sham acupuncture."
- **The sham problem:** a placebo control must be inert AND indistinguishable; sham acupuncture reliably fails one. Minimal needling at non-points "may cause physiological effects," so a null true-vs-sham result is ambiguous. Non-penetrating devices fix inertness but "it is impossible to blind the study investigators." Present as an UNRESOLVED design problem both camps invoke - not a win for either side.

**Herbal evidence quality:**
- **NCCIH:** studies "have had mixed results"; "Because many studies have been of poor quality, no firm conclusions can be made about their effectiveness."
- **Cochrane overview** (Hu et al, PLOS ONE 2011): of **58 Cochrane reviews** of Chinese herbal medicines, **only 1 reached a positive conclusion**; 27 (53%) "might be benefit" but quality-limited; 23 (45%) inconclusive. Across **671 primary studies: 16% adequate sequence generation, 7% adequate allocation concealment**.
- **AMSTAR-2 survey:** **96.6% (143/148)** of CHM systematic reviews were critically low methodological quality; exactly 1 was high.
- **Publication bias** (Vickers, Goyal, Harland & Rees, Control Clin Trials 1998;19:159-166): in a Medline sample **1966-1995**, every acupuncture trial from China, Japan, Hong Kong, Taiwan was positive; all-intervention figures China 99%, Russia/USSR 97%, Taiwan 95%, Japan 89%, England 75%. "No trial published in China or Russia/USSR found a test treatment to be ineffective." ALWAYS date it: 1998 analysis of pre-1996 literature; the 100% figure is the acupuncture subset only.

**Artemisinin - the honest positive:**
- **2015 Nobel divided**: half jointly to Campbell + Ōmura (roundworm), **the other half to 屠呦呦 Tu Youyou ALONE** for antimalarial therapy. First Chinese citizen to win the medicine Nobel. NOT a three-way TCM prize - Campbell/Ōmura's work is unrelated.
- **Project 523**, launched **23 May 1967** (hence the name); ~500 scientists, ~60 institutions; Tu made group head **1969**, aged 39.
- **葛洪 Ge Hong, 《肘后备急方》 c. 340 CE**: qinghao 青蒿 entry says **cold soaking, not hot decoction** - "take two sheng of water for soaking it, wring it out, take the juice" - which told Tu heated extraction was destroying the compound, leading to **low-temperature ether extraction**. Neutral ether extract **№ 191** achieved **100% inhibition** of P. berghei in mice, **October 1971**; pure artemisinin isolated 1972.
- **The honest lesson:** the classical text supplied a **screening hypothesis and a processing hint**. Everything after was conventional pharmacology. Validates **ethnobotany as a search heuristic** - not qi, meridians, or pattern differentiation. WHO discourages Artemisia annua tea (sub-therapeutic dosing drives resistance).

**Safety flags:**
- **Belgian aristolochic acid nephropathy:** Vanherweghem et al, **Lancet 13 Feb 1993** - nine women under 50 in renal failure, all from ONE Brussels slimming clinic. Mechanism = **botanical substitution**: formula changed May 1990, *Stephania tetrandra* (汉防己) replaced by *Aristolochia fangchi* (广防己) **because both share the common name 防己 fangji**. **100+ patients** affected; ~70+ needed dialysis or transplant.
- **Cancer:** Nortier et al, NEJM 2000;342:1686-92 - of **39 patients** with prophylactic nephroureterectomy, **18 urothelial carcinomas, prevalence 46% (CI 29-62%)**; dose-response 8/24 below 200g cumulative vs 10/15 above.
- **IARC two stages - get this right:** **Vol 82 (2002)** = herbal remedies containing *Aristolochia* species are **Group 1**; naturally occurring aristolochic acid MIXTURES were Group 2A. **Vol 100A (2012)** upgraded: "Aristolochic acid is carcinogenic to humans (Group 1)." Saying "IARC classified aristolochic acid Group 1 in 2002" is WRONG.
- **Regulation:** UK prohibited *Aristolochia* in herbal medicines **1999** (SI 1999/2109, in force 28 July 1999); MHRA: constituents are "nephrotoxic, carcinogenic, and mutagenic - even at very small doses." EU bans ~1999-2001. US FDA letters May 2000, **Consumer Advisory 11 April 2001**, Import Alert 54-10. **Enforcement gap:** NEJM 2003;349:1576-77, "Aristolochic Acid, an Herbal Carcinogen, Sold on the Web after FDA Alert."
- **Contamination:** Ko RJ, "Adulterants in Asian Patent Medicines," **NEJM 1998;339:847** - California DHS analysed **260 Asian patent medicines**: **at least 83 (32%)** contained undeclared pharmaceuticals or heavy metals; **24 lead (median 30 ppm), 36 arsenic (median 180 ppm), 35 mercury (median 329 ppm)**; 17 others had undeclared drugs (ephedrine, chlorpheniramine, methyltestosterone, phenacetin).
- **Herb-drug interactions:** best-documented case is **St John's wort** (label it accurately as a WESTERN herb illustrating the mechanism). FDA Public Health Advisory **10 Feb 2000** after an NIH study in 8 volunteers showed it **cut indinavir AUC by a mean 57%** via CYP3A4 induction; NCCIH documents interactions with cyclosporine, oral contraceptives, warfarin, digoxin, and implication in acute organ rejection. **DO NOT assert danshen/warfarin** - not verified to an authoritative standard.
- **NCCIH on Chinese herbal products:** found contaminated with "undeclared plant or animal material; drugs...; heavy metals (such as arsenic, lead, and cadmium); pesticides or...sulfites... or incorrect herbs, some of which have caused organ damage."

**ICD-11:**
- Adopted **WHA72, May 2019**; **in force 1 January 2022**. Includes **Chapter 26, "Supplementary Chapter Traditional Medicine Conditions - Module 1" (TM1)**, codes SA00-SJ1Z: ~150 disorders, ~196 patterns, harmonising Chinese/Japanese/Korean classifications, for **optional dual coding ALONGSIDE - never instead of - a conventional ICD-11 diagnosis**.
- **WHO's own framing** (spokesperson Gregory Hartl, verbatim): "Inclusion in the ICD is not a recommendation of a treatment's efficacy; inclusion acknowledges that a disease or symptom or condition exists and that it should be measured."
- **Criticism side 1:** *Scientific American* editorial, **1 April 2019**: ICD inclusion "recklessly equates them with medicines that have undergone clinical trials"; "Data supporting the effectiveness of most traditional remedies are scant, at best."
- **Criticism side 2:** *Nature* editorial, **5 June 2019** - breadth risks legitimising the underlying philosophy; likely to "fuel the expanding sales of largely unproven treatments." (Paywalled; do not quote at length beyond this.)
- **Counter-position:** proponents argue standardised coding is a PREREQUISITE for generating comparable safety and effectiveness data.

**Translations:**
- **Gold standard:** Unschuld & Tessenow with Zheng Jinsheng, *Huang Di Nei Jing Su Wen: An Annotated Translation*, 2 vols, **UC Press 2011**, ~1,560 pp. Plus Unschuld, *Huang Di Nei Jing Ling Shu*, **UC Press 2016** (all 81 Lingshu chapters). Supporting: Unschuld 2003 study volume; Tessenow & Unschuld, *A Dictionary of the Huang Di nei jing su wen*, 2008.
- **Historical artefact:** Ilza Veith, *The Yellow Emperor's Classic of Internal Medicine*, UC Press **1949**, **chapters 1-34 only**. Judged harshly by later scholars; the title itself is a mistranslation - 内经 = "inner canon," not "internal medicine" as a clinical specialty. Do NOT invent an Unschuld quote criticising it.
- Avoid uncited wellness-market "translations" entirely.

**Cultural context:**
- **中医四大经典 - TWO competing lists, both in use.** List A (traditional): 《黄帝内经》《难经》《伤寒杂病论》《神农本草经》. List B (modern curricular, used by the national 中医经典能力等级考试): 《黄帝内经》《伤寒论》《金匮要略》+ 温病学. Why they differ: 张仲景's 《伤寒杂病论》 was later split into 《伤寒论》 and 《金匮要略》 - List A counts one book, List B counts two. **In every version the Neijing is #1** - that is the safe claim.
- **Still examinable today:** 内经选读 is a 必修课 (required course) for the TCM major; the **2020 revision of the 医师资格考试大纲（中医）** broke out **中医经典** as a standalone licensing exam module. A dedicated 全国中医经典能力等级考试 (three levels) launched 2020. Safe striking claim: **"A 2,000-year-old text is still a graded, examinable requirement for becoming a licensed doctor in China today."**
- **Tang 太医署** (often called the world's earliest state medical school): students studied 《素问》 and 《黄帝针经》 under 《唐六典》's exam regime - **博士月一试，太医令、丞季一试，太常丞年终总试** (monthly, quarterly, year-end), expelled after nine years of failure.

## Must-hedge list (carried into every page)
1. "81+81 has always been the structure" - it is Wang Bing's 762 CE arrangement.
2. "This is what the Han text said" - no surviving version is identical to the Han text; some passages are Tang additions from Wang Bing's red ink.
3. Lung emotion: ch.5 says 忧, textbooks often say 悲. Name both.
4. 长夏 is from ch.22, not ch.5 - present as a patch.
5. "Scholars prefer 'phases'" - they agree "elements" is wrong, disagree on the replacement.
6. 主运化 is the later textbook term; the Neijing says 倉廩之官.
7. 有名而无形 is the Nanjing, not the Neijing.
8. "Acupuncture works" / "it's placebo" - both overclaims. State both effect sizes, let the gap speak.
9. The sham critique is an unresolved design problem, not ammunition.
10. The 1998 publication-bias figure: date it, and note 100% is the acupuncture subset.
11. Tu Youyou won her half ALONE; the Nobel validates ethnobotany, not qi.
12. IARC: 2002 = plants Group 1; 2012 = the acid itself Group 1.
13. Do not assert danshen/warfarin. Use St John's wort, labelled as Western.
14. UNESCO copy is **Yuan 1339**, not Ming.
15. 上医治未病 three-tier saying is **Sun Simiao**, not the Neijing.
16. Anything clinical pairs with NCCIH: "Don't use TCM to replace or delay seeking conventional care."
